What litigation budgets look like now

Legal spend benchmarks from the Association of Corporate Counsel and Norton Rose Fulbright's corporate counsel survey.

Corporate legal departments spent 2026 doing more with less, and the ACC numbers say so twice over: spend fell to its lowest share of revenue in six years while each in-house lawyer picked up more of the company to cover — 367 employees apiece, up from 300 five years earlier. That is not a contradiction. It is the bargain legal departments have struck: tighter budgets, leaner teams, and a growing share of work pushed to outside firms, whose slice of spend ticked up even as the total shrank.

How to read these numbers

Included: the ACC Law Department Management Benchmarking Reports (2023, 2025, 2026 — 576 departments across 45 countries in 2026) and the Norton Rose Fulbright Annual Litigation Trends Survey. Excluded: Thomson Reuters' own legal spend reports, which could not be verified from a public primary source this session, and law-firm rate surveys that price hours rather than budgets. ACC figures reach this page partly through the ACC's own press release and trade reporting of the full report, and are labeled as such.

Disclaimer: Benchmarks are medians across departments of very different sizes and industries; a median legal spend figure describes no actual department, and regulated or IP-heavy industries run well above it. Figures on this page are aggregated public estimates assembled for information only.
